# Deep Learning IndabaX South Sudan 2026

**IndabaX South Sudan 2026** brought together students, developers, researchers and founders in Juba to learn, discuss and build with artificial intelligence. It was organised by Scenius Hub as part of the [Deep Learning Indaba](https://deeplearningindaba.com/) movement, which strengthens machine learning and artificial intelligence across Africa.

This repository holds the articles, notebooks and learning resources from the programme.

## The programme

This year's programme took participants through four stages: **Learn → Engage → Build → Showcase**.

| Date | Stage | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24 Sept 2026 | Learn | Hands-on *Introduction to Machine Learning* workshop at Twenty11 Hub |
| 24–30 Sept 2026 | Build | Food security forecasting hackathon on Zindi |
| 1 Oct 2026 | Engage + Showcase | Talks, founders panel and exhibitions at Scenius Hub |

### Learn: first steps into machine learning

It began on 24 September 2026 with a one-day, in-person *Introduction to Machine Learning* workshop at Twenty11 Hub. **96 people registered.** Most were students (71%) and complete beginners in ML (68%), and 25% were women.

Working in Google Colab, participants went from "What is machine learning?" to training their own models. They predicted crop yields and household food security risk using a dataset based on South Sudan's ten states. They also learned how to use Zindi, including how to download data and submit predictions, so they would be ready for the hackathon.

> "From beginner to being able to build a mini model." — Alek Madut Malual

7 of the 8 participants (87.5%) who gave feedback said they now feel more confident about machine learning.

### Build: forecasting food security on Zindi

From 24 to 30 September, participants put their new skills to work in the IndabaX South Sudan hackathon on Zindi. The challenge was to build models that predict **food security risk across South Sudan's 79 counties**, using data from South Sudan National Bureau of Statistics reports.

Students from the University of Juba, Starford International University and other institutions took part individually or in teams. They cleaned real data, compared models and submitted predictions to a live leaderboard. For many, it was their first data science competition.

> "I came in just wanting to participate, learned a lot about data, ML, and food-security forecasting, and actually got to build and submit a real model. As a young tech guy from South Sudan, seeing my work compete on a real platform was a big motivation." — Chakuoth Koy Mayiel, final-year Computer Science student, Starford International University

### Engage: talks and a founders panel on 1 October

On Thursday, 1 October 2026, the community met at Scenius Hub in Juba. **More than 100 participants** joined, including students, developers, researchers, founders and industry professionals.

**Inside AI: how it works, what it can do, and where it falls short.** Poni Henry, Lead Organiser of IndabaX South Sudan, led the technical session. She went from a simple definition of AI to how machines learn, neural networks and how ChatGPT predicts the next word, and trained a crop-yield model live in Google Colab. The session ended with AI's limits: bias, hallucinations, poor local fit, and the lack of data in South Sudanese languages such as Dinka, Nuer, Bari and Juba Arabic.

**Start Now: building AI and ML skills as a student.** Chol Daniel Deng Dau, a software engineer and Technical Project Lead at Businesslab IT Services, shared a five-step learning path: Python, data handling, maths as you need it, ML fundamentals, then deep learning. He showed how his team built Unesis AI, which translates medical information into African languages, in 48 hours. It won 1st place at the ALU × Meta Hackathon 2024. His challenge to the room: pick one problem, build one project, and start this month.

**AI in Banking: gains, risks and access to finance.** Mabil Deng of Stanbic Bank South Sudan gave a lightning talk on what AI has done for banking. He covered faster services, fraud detection and better credit decisions, as well as the risks: biased lending, privacy and AI-enabled scams. His main point was that AI in finance must be governed, and sensitive customer data should never be pasted into AI tools without safeguards. For South Sudan, he said, the biggest opportunity is reaching people the formal banking system has left out.

**Founders panel: building for first-time users.** Moderated by Gaso Grace Pascal, the panel brought together Nelson Kwaje (founder of Scenius Hub, CEO of Digital Rights Frontlines), John Marit (founder of Mobilaws) and Johnson Achuk (founder of AcademiaPro). They talked about a market where most customers are first-time digital users, and where trust has to come before features. All three already use AI in their work, from checking more than 15,000 documents a month to cleaning 17,000 spreadsheet entries in minutes. Their biggest needs: a more stable economy and real access to seed funding. Kwaje encouraged founders to learn openly from failure: *"We do launches but we don't do funerals."*

### Showcase: local solutions on display

Between sessions, participants explored exhibitions of AI and tech products built in and for South Sudan:

- **AcademiaPro:** a school management system built only for South Sudanese schools. It follows the country's own school structure and curriculum, and takes paperwork off teachers so they can focus on students.
- **Strategos Edge:** a private, local AI system for businesses and organisations. It analyses their own documents and data, such as sales, inventory, expenses and reports, without needing a constant internet connection. It answers management questions, spots operational issues and bases every answer on the evidence provided, for places where connectivity, privacy and access to cloud AI are real challenges.
- **Bilingual AI humanoid robot:** a prototype humanoid robot that speaks English and Arabic. It was built to promote peace, support digital farming and inspire interest in STEM education, showing how AI can serve communities in South Sudan.
- **Pathway AI:** an AI-powered career guidance system that recommends fields of study based on students' skills, interests and academic strengths.
- **Research poster on AI disinformation:** how false information created with AI spreads in South Sudan, and why it matters to verify before sharing.

The day also celebrated the hackathon participants. **Final winners will be announced once the code review of top submissions is complete.**

## What's next

IndabaX South Sudan 2026 showed that the talent is already here. What young builders need now is data, compute, connectivity and a community that keeps meeting. Next, Scenius Hub plans to turn the food security forecasting work into an **open-source community project**, so the models and insights keep growing after the competition. We will also continue hosting meetups and trainings throughout the year.
