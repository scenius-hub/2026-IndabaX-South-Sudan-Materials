# Free Courses: A Learning Path

These courses are arranged in the order we recommend taking them. You don't need to finish every course in a stage before moving on; one good course per stage is enough to progress.

> **Cost note:** All courses below are free to access. Some platforms (like Coursera) charge for certificates, but you can usually **audit** the course for free or apply for **financial aid**, which is often approved for learners in South Sudan.

---

## Stage 1: Python and programming basics

Python is the main language for AI and machine learning. Start here if you've never coded, or if you're unsure of the basics.

| Course | Provider | Level | Time | Why take it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Kaggle Learn: Python](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/python) | Kaggle | Beginner | ~5 hours | Short, practical lessons with exercises that run in the browser |
| [CS50's Introduction to Programming with Python](https://cs50.harvard.edu/python/) | Harvard University | Beginner | ~10 weeks | One of the best-taught programming courses anywhere; free certificate |
| [Python for Everybody](https://www.py4e.com/) | Dr Charles Severance, University of Michigan | Beginner | ~8 weeks | Very gentle; good if CS50 feels too fast. Materials can be downloaded |

**You're ready to move on when you can:** write functions, use loops and if-statements, and work with lists and dictionaries.

## Stage 2: Data skills and maths foundations

Machine learning is mostly about data. You need to load, clean and explore it, and understand a little statistics.

| Course | Provider | Level | Time | Why take it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Kaggle Learn: Pandas](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/pandas) | Kaggle | Beginner | ~4 hours | The pandas skills used in the IndabaX workshop notebook |
| [Kaggle Learn: Data Visualization](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/data-visualization) | Kaggle | Beginner | ~4 hours | Turn data into charts that tell a story |
| [Kaggle Learn: Data Cleaning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/data-cleaning) | Kaggle | Beginner | ~4 hours | Real-world data (like NBS reports) is messy; this teaches you to fix it |
| [Statistics and Probability](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability) | Khan Academy | Beginner | Self-paced | Mean, variance, distributions and probability, explained simply |
| [Essence of Linear Algebra](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/linear-algebra) | 3Blue1Brown | Beginner–Intermediate | ~3 hours of video | Visual intuition for vectors and matrices, the maths inside ML models |

**You're ready to move on when you can:** load a CSV into pandas, filter and group it, handle missing values, and make a basic chart.

## Stage 3: Machine learning fundamentals

This is where the IndabaX workshop fits. These courses go deeper into the same ideas: training, testing, loss and evaluation.

| Course | Provider | Level | Time | Why take it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Machine Learning Crash Course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course) | Google | Beginner | ~15 hours | Fast, practical introduction with interactive visuals |
| [Kaggle Learn: Intro to Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-machine-learning) | Kaggle | Beginner | ~3 hours | Builds decision trees and random forests, like the workshop |
| [Kaggle Learn: Intermediate Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intermediate-machine-learning) | Kaggle | Intermediate | ~4 hours | Missing values, pipelines, cross-validation, XGBoost |
| [Machine Learning Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/machine-learning-introduction) | Andrew Ng, DeepLearning.AI and Stanford (Coursera) | Beginner–Intermediate | ~2–3 months | The most popular ML course in the world. Audit for free or apply for financial aid |
| [Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) | University of Helsinki | Beginner, non-technical | ~6 weeks | Great for non-programmers who want to understand what AI is and isn't |

**You're ready to move on when you can:** split data into training and test sets, train a model with scikit-learn, and explain whether it's overfitting.

## How to finish a course (and not quit halfway)

1. **Pick one course per stage.** Switching courses often feels productive but slows you down.
2. **Schedule it.** Two fixed sessions a week beat "whenever I have time."
3. **Code along.** Pause videos and type the code yourself instead of just watching.
4. **Find a study partner** through the Scenius Hub Tech Community.
5. **Build a mini project at the end of each stage**, even a small one, using a dataset from [datasets.md](datasets.md).
